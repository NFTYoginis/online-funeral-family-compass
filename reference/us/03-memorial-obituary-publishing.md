# 03 — Memorial / obituary / tribute publishing (US)

The product-touch surface of online funeral services. This is what the family is on the platform to do. The compass cites this file whenever the family asks about memorial-page setup, obituary publication, privacy controls, or platform-specific features.

## The named US platforms families typically use

| Platform | URL | Free tier? | What it specializes in |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Ever Loved** | [everloved.com](https://everloved.com) | Yes — tribute pages, fundraising, planning tools | All-in-one: memorial page + fundraising + funeral-planning checklist |
| **Forever Missed** | [forevermissed.com](https://forevermissed.com) | Yes — basic tribute page; paid tier removes ads, longer hosting, custom URL | Long-form memorial pages, photo and video galleries |
| **Tribute Archive** (formerly Tribute) | [tributearchive.com](https://tributearchive.com) | Distributed via funeral homes (the funeral home pays) | Funeral-home-distributed tribute pages; family-side is free |
| **GatheringUs** | [gatheringus.com](https://gatheringus.com) | Mixed — basic features free, full event-coordination paid | Virtual memorial service coordination (streaming + memorial page combined) |
| **Legacy.com** | [legacy.com](https://legacy.com) | Newspaper-syndicated obituaries | Aggregates obituaries from local newspapers; "Guest Book" is the tribute layer |
| **Find a Grave** | [findagrave.com](https://findagrave.com) | Yes (free, community-edited) | Cemetery-records cross-reference; genealogy-oriented; not primarily a service for the recently-bereaved |

**Note:** Platform features and pricing change; verify directly with the platform when the family is about to commit. The compass should not quote specific paid-tier prices that could be stale — point to the platform's pricing page.

## What's standard across platforms (the family can expect)

- **A tribute page** — name, dates, photo, life summary (the family writes; templates often provided)
- **Photo galleries** — drag-and-drop upload, captions, often unlimited on free tier
- **Tribute messages / "guest book" / "memories"** — visitors leave written messages; family can moderate
- **Candle-lighting** — visitors click to "light a candle"; pure ritual, no functional consequence
- **Privacy / visibility controls** — typically three tiers:
  - **Public** — anyone with the URL can view (and visitors can find via search)
  - **Unlisted** — only people with the URL can find it; not search-indexed
  - **Private / password-protected** — visitors need a password the family shares
- **Donation links** — most platforms integrate with charitable-giving (in lieu of flowers); the platform usually takes a processing fee
- **Obituary publication** — some platforms cross-publish to Legacy.com or partner newspaper networks
- **Live-streaming or virtual-service integration** — see [04-virtual-memorial-service.md](04-virtual-memorial-service.md)

## What's free vs. paid

| Feature | Typically free | Typically paid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tribute page itself | Yes | — |
| Unlimited photos | Often yes | Sometimes capped on free; paid removes cap |
| Long-term hosting | Often capped at 1–2 years free | Paid extends to 5 years / lifetime |
| Ad-free display | No (free pages show ads) | Yes (paid removes) |
| Custom URL | No | Yes |
| Live-stream hosting | Sometimes | More commonly paid |
| Print-on-demand keepsakes (memorial books, prayer cards) | No | Yes |
| Donation processing | Available on free, but fees apply | Reduced fees on paid |

The family should NOT feel pressured into the paid tier on day one. Free pages serve the purpose. If they later want longer hosting or a custom URL, that's a month-2 decision, not a today decision.

## What to publish vs. defer

### TODAY / THIS WEEK: safe to publish

- Name, dates, photo
- A brief life summary (the family writes — see template below)
- Funeral or memorial service details (date, time, location, livestream link)
- "In lieu of flowers, donations to [charity]" if the family has chosen one
- The memorial page URL — share via email or text to those who should know

### Defer (and the family should understand why)

- **Home address** — visible on a public page invites theft during the funeral. The funeral or memorial service location is fine; the family home address is not.
- **Children's names with surnames** — privacy for surviving minor children matters.
- **The full will, financial details, or estate information** — never, regardless of timing.
- **Photos of specific possessions** ("Mom's pearls," "Dad's watch") — invites theft of identifiable items.
- **Specific cause-of-death details** if the family hasn't decided on this — once published, it's hard to retract.

### The "in lieu of flowers" decision

A real decision, not a default. Options:
- Flowers welcomed — say nothing about it
- Flowers welcomed AND donations to [charity] — name the charity, with link
- Donations only, no flowers — common request; specify charity
- Donations to the family / GoFundMe — use a fundraising platform like Ever Loved or GoFundMe, NOT a personal Venmo for tax/transparency reasons

## Obituary publication options

| Channel | Cost (US, 2025) | Audience | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Local newspaper print + online** | $200–$1,200 depending on length, photo, edition (weekday vs. Sunday) | The family's immediate community, older readers | Some newspapers require a funeral home to submit on behalf of the family; this is a soft rule, push back if needed |
| **Legacy.com (newspaper-syndicated)** | Free if your newspaper participates; $200+ if family-paid | Aggregate audience nationally | Most US dailies syndicate here |
| **Online memorial page (Ever Loved, Forever Missed, etc.)** | Free | Anyone the family shares the URL with | No length limit |
| **Funeral home website obituary** | Usually free (included in services) | Funeral home's local community | Often cross-posted to Legacy.com automatically |
| **Social media (the deceased's Facebook, family's social accounts)** | Free | The deceased's network | Facebook has a separate "Memorialization" feature — see below |

The family does NOT need to pick one. They can do all of them. The right answer is usually: free online memorial page (always) + funeral home obituary (usually included) + one local newspaper if it matters to their community.

## Facebook / social media memorialization

Facebook has a formal **Memorialization** process: the account stays up, but is marked "Remembering [Name]" and stops appearing in birthday reminders, friend suggestions, and "On This Day" prompts. The family can request memorialization at [facebook.com/help/contact/305593649477238](https://www.facebook.com/help/contact/305593649477238) — they'll need to provide a copy of the death certificate.

A "legacy contact" (set up by the deceased while alive) can manage a memorialized account. If no legacy contact, the family can request:
- Memorialization (account stays up, posts visible to existing friends)
- Removal (account deleted entirely)

Instagram and LinkedIn have similar but distinct processes. Family should batch these in month 1, not week 1.

## A life-summary template the compass can offer

```
[Full name], [age], of [city, state], passed [peacefully / unexpectedly / after a long illness] on [date], at [home / hospital / hospice].

[He / She / They] was born on [birthday] in [birthplace] to [parents' names].

[1–3 sentences on early life: school, hometown details.]

[1–3 sentences on adult life: career, marriage, children, where they lived.]

[1–2 sentences on what they loved: hobbies, passions, what people who knew them best would say.]

[Survivors: spouse, children, siblings, grandchildren — by name.]

[Predeceased by: parents, siblings, spouse if applicable.]

[Service details: date, time, location, livestream link.]

[In lieu of flowers: donations to ___, link.]
```

Families often appreciate having this template. They don't have to follow it; it gives them a place to start when they're staring at a blank field.
