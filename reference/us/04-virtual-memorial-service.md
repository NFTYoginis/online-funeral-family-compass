# 04 — Virtual memorial service / livestream (US)

The "online" in "online funeral" operationally. The compass cites this file when the family asks about livestreaming, remote attendees, or hybrid in-person/virtual service planning.

## The two operational shapes

| Shape | What it is | When it fits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Pure virtual service** | The entire service happens online — clergy/officiant + family on Zoom/equivalent, no physical gathering | Geographically scattered family; pandemic-era or health-conscious families; small intimate services; cost-constrained families |
| **Hybrid (in-person + livestream)** | Service happens at the funeral home / church / graveside; a livestream is broadcast for remote attendees | Most common since 2020; lets out-of-town family attend without traveling; typical default for traditional service + cremation paths |

A "memorial service" (held weeks or months after the death, not at the funeral home) is also commonly hybrid — the family rents a venue or hosts at home, with a streaming link for those who can't travel.

## Platform options for streaming

### Through the funeral home

Most US funeral homes now offer livestreaming as part of their services (often included; sometimes $100–$300 add-on). The family should ASK — many don't volunteer it.

- The funeral home uses a fixed camera (usually a smartphone on a tripod or a dedicated camera) pointed at the chapel/service area.
- The stream goes to a private URL (sometimes password-protected) the family shares.
- The recording is usually available to download or rewatch for 30–90 days.
- Quality is variable — confirm the funeral home has done this before and ask to see a sample if it matters.

### Self-hosted (Zoom / Google Meet / Teams)

The family runs the stream themselves with a smartphone, tablet, or laptop.

- **Pros:** Full control, recording is in your possession, free for short sessions
- **Cons:** Someone in the family is doing tech support during the service; audio quality from the back of a chapel is often poor; if the wifi is bad, the stream is bad
- **Best when:** The service is small, the venue has decent wifi, and a younger family member can run the stream

### Dedicated virtual-memorial platforms

- **GatheringUs** ([gatheringus.com](https://gatheringus.com)) specializes in this — they coordinate the stream, send invitations, handle the recording. Paid service; family-supplied content (officiant, family members speaking) on a platform that handles the logistics.
- **OneRoom** ([oneroomstreaming.com](https://oneroomstreaming.com)) — many funeral homes use this as their streaming backend.

### Memorial-page integration

Some online memorial platforms (Ever Loved, Forever Missed) can embed a livestream URL on the memorial page itself, so visitors find both the tribute and the stream in one place. Confirm the platform supports this and test it before the day.

## What to plan, by day

### One week before service

- **Confirm with the funeral home** whether they're streaming and on what platform.
- **Test the stream** if possible — funeral home can do a 5-minute test the day before.
- **Decide who's running the stream** if it's family-hosted — designate ONE person, not "whoever's nearby."
- **Decide on privacy** — public link, password, or invitation-only.
- **Write the invitation message** — typically: "We're holding a service for [name] on [date] at [time, time zone]. In-person attendees are welcome at [address]; remote attendees can join the livestream at [URL] (password: [if any])."

### Day before

- **Send the invitation** to the people who should receive it. Email and text both — older relatives often miss email.
- **Verify the livestream URL works** by clicking it from a phone (not the same wifi as the source).
- **Confirm time zone explicitly** — "2pm Pacific (5pm Eastern)" not just "2pm."

### Day of

- **Start the stream 10 minutes early** so remote attendees can confirm they're in the right place.
- **Don't read the chat aloud** during the service — it's a service, not a webinar.
- **Record the service** — most platforms auto-record; confirm.
- **Acknowledge remote attendees** at the start, briefly. They came; they should be welcomed.

### Day after

- **Share the recording URL** with attendees who couldn't watch live, via the memorial page or email.
- **Note for the future:** Some families later regret the recording; others treasure it. Don't decide on permanence today.

## Common failure modes the compass should warn about

1. **The wifi at the venue is too weak for streaming.** This is the #1 failure. Verify ahead, or hotspot from a phone.
2. **The audio is muffled.** Funeral home cameras often have weak microphones. A clip-on lavalier mic for the officiant, plugged into the camera or a phone, fixes this.
3. **The link is wrong in the invitation.** Test the actual link in the actual email by clicking it before sending the email widely.
4. **No one is muting attendees.** If 30 remote attendees join unmuted, the service is unwatchable. Use webinar-mode or designate a moderator.
5. **The recording is lost.** Confirm where the recording lives BEFORE the service; many platforms delete cloud recordings after 30 days unless the host downloads them.
6. **Time-zone confusion.** Always state the time zone explicitly in invitations.

## What this is NOT

- A "memorial Zoom call" two weeks after the funeral, hosted by the family, is a virtual memorial gathering — different shape, more conversational, often more useful than the streamed-funeral itself. The compass can suggest this as a separate event.
- A YouTube live-stream made public to the world — most families don't actually want this. Default to private/unlisted.

## Sample family script for asking the funeral home

> "Do you offer livestreaming for the service? If yes — what platform do you use, can I see a sample stream from a recent service, what's the cost, who runs it on your end, and how long is the recording available afterward?"

If the answer is "we don't do livestreaming," the family can:
1. Use a different funeral home (price-comparison is also a Funeral Rule right)
2. Run the stream themselves from a phone in the back of the chapel
3. Hire a dedicated service like GatheringUs to coordinate
