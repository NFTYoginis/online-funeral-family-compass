# 02 — FTC Funeral Rule (US)

The single most consequential consumer-protection regulation a bereaved US family has at the funeral home. Enacted **1984**, amended **1994** (the "Funeral Industry Practices" rule, 16 C.F.R. Part 453). Applies to all funeral providers in all 50 states.

The compass cites this file whenever pricing, contracts, or funeral-home conduct comes up. Families almost universally don't know they have these rights.

## What the Rule entitles a family to

### 1. The General Price List (GPL)

> The funeral provider must give you a General Price List (GPL), in writing, that you may keep, that itemizes the price of every funeral good and service the provider offers.

- Must be offered **at the start** of any discussion of funeral arrangements.
- Must be given **on request** to anyone who asks in person, regardless of whether they're a customer.
- Must list the **specific items**, not just packages.
- Failure to provide is a Funeral Rule violation and can be reported to the FTC at [reportfraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov).

### 2. Price disclosure by phone

> The funeral provider must give you accurate price information over the telephone for any specific item or service the family asks about.

- The provider can NOT require the family to come in for "the personal consultation" before disclosing prices.
- The provider can NOT require the family's name, address, or other identifying information as a precondition to price disclosure.

### 3. Itemized selection — no package coercion

> You have the right to choose only the goods and services you want — and the funeral provider may not condition the sale of any item on the purchase of another item, unless required by state or federal law.

In practice, the only items that may be lawfully required are:
- The **basic services fee** (the funeral home's overhead — present in every funeral, every state).
- Items required by **state law** (varies — none required in most states for direct cremation or direct burial).
- Items required for the **specific arrangement chosen** (e.g., a casket is needed for a viewing, an embalming may be needed if a viewing is held more than a few days after death).

Things that are NOT legally required in any state:
- **Embalming** for direct cremation or direct burial.
- **A casket for cremation** (a rigid cremation container is used; the casket, if any, is for viewing only).
- **An "outer burial container" / vault** (federal law doesn't require it; some cemeteries do — ask the specific cemetery, not the funeral home).
- **A specific provider's casket** — the family can buy one elsewhere; see Rule 5.

### 4. Embalming consent

> You don't have to pay for embalming you didn't approve, unless it's required by state law.

- The funeral provider must obtain the family's **explicit permission** before embalming, except in the rare case where state law requires it.
- The family has the right to choose an arrangement (direct cremation, direct burial, immediate-burial with a closed-casket service) that does not require embalming.

### 5. Casket / outside-purchase rule

> You have the right to provide your own casket or urn, purchased from a third party (online, from Costco, from a casket retailer), and the funeral provider may not refuse to use it, and may not charge a handling fee for it.

This rule was added in the 1994 amendment specifically to address funeral-home gatekeeping of the casket market. A casket bought online for $800 is operationally identical to one sold at the funeral home for $3,000 — the family can buy elsewhere and have it shipped to the funeral home.

### 6. The Statement of Funeral Goods and Services Selected

After the family makes selections, the funeral provider must give them an **itemized statement**, in writing, that lists every item and service selected and its price, and the total. This is the contract.

The family should:
- **Review it line by line** before signing.
- **Compare against the GPL** to confirm consistency.
- **Refuse to sign** anything they don't understand or didn't authorize.
- **Get a copy** of the signed statement, kept by the family.

## What the Rule does NOT cover

- **Cemetery services** (plot purchase, opening/closing fees, headstone) — different industry, different (much weaker) consumer protections. Cemeteries are regulated state-by-state, often inadequately.
- **Pre-need funeral plans** — partially covered by state pre-need-trust laws; consumer protections vary materially by state. Be skeptical of pre-need sales pressure; Funeral Consumers Alliance has guidance.
- **Online memorial platforms** — not the funeral industry per se; covered by general consumer-protection law and platform terms of service.

## How to invoke the Rule when needed

Sample family scripts the compass can suggest:

- **Requesting the GPL:** "Per the FTC Funeral Rule, I'd like a copy of your General Price List in writing, please."
- **Refusing a package:** "Per the FTC Funeral Rule, I'd like to select items individually. Please give me an itemized total for [list]."
- **Bringing a third-party casket:** "Per the FTC Funeral Rule, we'll be providing our own casket from [retailer]. Please don't include a casket on the bill, and please confirm you won't charge a handling fee."
- **Reporting a violation:** [reportfraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov) — file a complaint with the FTC. The state attorney general's consumer protection office may also accept funeral-industry complaints.

## STOP-flag situations (irreversible commitments)

Before the family signs:

- **A pre-payment for "future services" beyond initial deposit** — STOP unless they fully understand the state's pre-need-trust protections.
- **A "waiver of further claims" or "release of liability"** — STOP. Don't sign waivers in the first week of arrangements.
- **A "package upgrade" presented as a discount** — STOP. Check the itemized GPL price for the same items.
- **A "we'll need to start immediately" pressure** — only cremation/burial authorization is truly time-sensitive; the rest of the contract can wait 24 hours.
