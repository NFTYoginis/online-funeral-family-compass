# 01 — Direct cremation vs traditional funeral logistics (US)

The first-week decision that drives every other decision. The compass uses this file when the family hasn't decided yet, when they've decided but haven't articulated tradeoffs, or when they're pushing back against a funeral home's package.

## The three operational paths

| Path | What it is | Typical cost range (US, 2025) | Typical timeline | Memorial service compatibility |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Direct cremation** | Cremation as soon as legally permitted; no embalming, no viewing, no service at the funeral home; remains returned to family in a basic container | $700–$3,000 | 3–10 days | Yes — memorial service held separately, on family's own schedule, any location |
| **Cremation with service** | Cremation + a visitation/viewing/service at the funeral home before or after | $2,000–$7,000 | 5–14 days | Built in |
| **Traditional burial** | Embalming + viewing + funeral service + casket + burial plot + outer container | $7,000–$15,000+ | 5–14 days | Built in |

Source for cost ranges: National Funeral Directors Association (NFDA) Member General Price List Survey, most recent annual report at [nfda.org](https://nfda.org). Costs vary 2-3x within the same metro area between funeral homes for identical services.

## What is universal across all 50 US states

- **The FTC Funeral Rule applies in all 50 states.** See [02-ftc-funeral-rule.md](02-ftc-funeral-rule.md). The family has the right to choose individual items, get a General Price List in writing, and refuse anything not legally required.
- **No US state requires embalming for direct cremation or direct burial.** Embalming is sometimes required if the body is being shipped across state lines, held for an extended viewing, or in specific public-health circumstances — but it is NOT a default legal requirement.
- **No US state requires a casket for cremation.** A rigid alternative cremation container (cardboard, wood, fiberboard) is what the crematorium actually uses; the casket, if any, is for the viewing only and can be rented from the funeral home.
- **All 50 states require a signed Cremation Authorization Form** from the legal next-of-kin before cremation. The hierarchy of next-of-kin is set by state law.

## What varies by state

- **Cremation waiting period.** Most states require 24–48 hours from time of death before cremation can occur. Exceptions: Hawaii (24hr), some states have no statutory wait, others can vary by county.
  - **California:** 48 hours (Calif. Health & Safety Code §7355).
  - **Texas:** 48 hours (Tex. Health & Safety Code §716.004).
  - **New York:** 48 hours.
  - **Florida:** 48 hours.
  - **For any other state**: tell the family "verify with the funeral home — they know your state's wait period."
- **Whether outer burial container (vault) is required.** Federal law does not require one; some cemeteries do; **California, Florida, Texas, and New York have no statewide requirement** but individual cemeteries within those states may. Always ask the cemetery.
- **Home funerals / family-directed funerals.** Legal in most states with varying paperwork burdens. Funeral Consumers Alliance (funerals.org) maintains a state-by-state guide at [funerals.org/your-rights/state-by-state-rights/](https://www.funerals.org/your-rights/state-by-state-rights/).
- **Witnessed cremation.** Some funeral homes/crematoriums in every state offer family witnessing; some don't. State law generally allows it; provider policy varies.

## Decision tradeoffs the compass surfaces

When the family hasn't decided:

| Factor | Direct cremation | Cremation with service | Traditional burial |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cost | Lowest | Middle | Highest |
| Timeline | Fastest (3–10 days) | Standard (5–14 days) | Standard (5–14 days) |
| In-person gathering | Possible later, off-site | Built in | Built in |
| Reversibility | None — cremation is irreversible | None for the cremation itself | Disinterment is technically possible but expensive and rare |
| Faith/cultural compatibility | Most Protestant denominations OK; Catholic Church now permits but with specific guidelines; Orthodox Judaism prohibits; most Muslim traditions prohibit | Same as direct | Most universally compatible across faiths |
| Out-of-town family travel pressure | Lowest (service can be scheduled later) | Medium (service is scheduled within ~1 week) | Medium (service is scheduled within ~1 week) |
| Memorial-page integration | Higher — the online memorial page often becomes the primary gathering surface | Equal | Equal |

When the family has decided, the compass does not re-litigate. It executes against the chosen path.

## Pre-signature flags (used in the STOP gate)

Before the family signs the **Statement of Funeral Goods and Services Selected** (the FTC-required itemized contract):

- **Embalming line item:** ask "is embalming legally required for what we've chosen?" — if the answer is "no, but recommended" they can decline.
- **Casket for cremation:** if direct cremation, refuse the casket up-sell. A rigid alternative container is what's used.
- **"Package" or "traditional service" pricing:** itemize. Federal law requires the funeral home to honor item-by-item selection.
- **Pre-payment beyond what's needed to start services:** rare to be necessary; many homes accept partial deposit + balance due at service completion.
- **Any "waiver of claims" or "release":** STOP. Don't sign waivers in the first week.
