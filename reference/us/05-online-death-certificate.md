# 05 — Death certificate ordering (US)

The first administrative document that gates everything else. The compass cites this file when the family asks how to get death certificates, how many they need, how long it takes, or what to do with them.

## Who issues a death certificate

In every US state, certified copies of a death certificate are issued by the **vital records office** of the state where the death occurred — NOT the state where the deceased lived, NOT the state where the family lives, NOT the federal government.

Each state has its own vital records office; most also delegate certificate issuance to **county/city** vital records offices for in-county deaths, which is often faster.

**For any other state:** search "vital records [state name]" — every state has a vital records office, and most have online ordering. **VitalChek** ([vitalchek.com](https://www.vitalchek.com)) is the authorized third-party ordering channel for most states, charging a per-certificate fee plus a service fee.

## How a family orders certified copies

Three channels in every state:

1. **In person** at the state vital records office or the county/city office where death occurred — typically the fastest if the office is nearby
2. **By mail** — fill out the state's application form, include payment (check or money order) and a copy of the requester's photo ID, mail it
3. **Online via VitalChek** (vitalchek.com) — the authorized third-party processor for most states; charges per certificate plus a service fee plus optional expedited shipping

The funeral director typically handles the **first** death certificate filing — they file the demographic information with the state, the medical examiner or attending physician fills in cause of death, and the state issues certified copies. The family then orders **additional** copies as needed.

## How many certified copies does a family need?

**Typical recommendation: 10–12 copies.** Each of these institutions usually wants an original (not a photocopy):

| Institution | Why it wants an original |
| --- | --- |
| **Social Security Administration** | To stop benefits and file survivor claims (often the funeral director files the first) |
| **Each bank** (checking, savings) | To close accounts or transfer to estate |
| **Each brokerage** | To transfer or close investment accounts |
| **Each life insurance company** | To process the claim |
| **Each pension plan** | To process survivor benefits |
| **The IRS** (with final tax return) | To file final and estate returns |
| **Probate court** (in the state of residence) | To open probate, file the will |
| **The deceased's employer** | For final paycheck, accrued PTO, benefits-survivor questions |
| **Each major credit card** | To close the account |
| **Vehicle title office** (state DMV) | To transfer ownership |
| **Each property title** (deeds) | To re-record title |
| **Veterans Affairs (if a veteran)** | To file VA survivor benefits and burial allowance claim |

**Order 12, not 6.** Re-ordering later costs more in time + fees than a single bulk order today.

## Typical processing times

- **Funeral director–filed initial certificate:** 7–21 days from death, depending on state and whether cause of death needed amendment
- **Online order via VitalChek:** 5–14 days typically; expedited overnight is available in most states for an extra fee
- **In-person at the issuing county office:** Often same-day or next-day, for in-person pickup
- **By mail:** 2–4 weeks
- **California specifically:** State office is slower (4–8 weeks); the county recorder's office in the county where the death occurred is much faster (often within a week)

These are typical numbers — state and county variation is wide. The compass should always say "verify with the state vital records office for current processing time" rather than promise a specific date.

## Typical cost per certified copy

- **State-issued, in person:** $10–$25 per copy
- **VitalChek online:** ~$24/copy + service fee + shipping (~$40–$50 all-in per copy for expedited)
- **By mail:** state base fee, no service charge

For 12 copies via the state office in person, expect $150–$300 total. Via VitalChek expedited, expect $400–$600 total.

## What's on a death certificate

- **Personal information:** name, date of birth, date of death, sex, race, marital status, occupation, parents' names
- **Cause of death:** filled in by the attending physician or medical examiner; can be amended later if needed (medical examiner's office handles)
- **Place of death:** address, type of facility
- **Informant:** the family member who provided the demographic information to the funeral director
- **Disposition:** burial, cremation, donation, or removal-from-state

The family doesn't need to know all of this in week 1. They need to know: order 12, expect 2–4 weeks, here's how.

## Federal records — Social Security Death Index

The Social Security Administration is notified by the funeral director (or by the family if not). Once SSA processes the death notification:

- Benefits stop (the family does NOT keep checks issued after the date of death — those must be returned)
- Survivors benefits become claimable (spouse, minor children, in some cases parents)
- The deceased's name enters the SSDI database used by background checks

SSA contact: [ssa.gov](https://www.ssa.gov), **1-800-772-1213**. The form for notifying SSA is **SSA-721 "Statement of Death by Funeral Director"** — the funeral director files this.

For survivor benefits, the family files **SSA-10** (widow/widower benefits) or **SSA-5** (parent's benefits) or **SSA-4** (child's benefits) — these require the death certificate.

## A common confusion: "certified" vs. "photocopy"

- **Certified copy** — printed on official state paper, with a raised seal or watermark, signed by the state registrar. Originals that institutions accept.
- **Photocopy** — useful for the family's records; NOT accepted by SSA, banks, insurers, courts, or the IRS.

When ordering, always ask for **certified copies**. Some states label them "certified copy with raised seal" or "long form" — confirm with the state office that what you're getting is what banks and the SSA will accept.
