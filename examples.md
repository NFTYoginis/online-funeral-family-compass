# Examples

Worked examples of how the Online Funeral Family Compass behaves. Each shows: the canonical intake, the four-column output, and (for examples 3 onward) a follow-on job in the same chat.

Format borrowed from [Grief Admin Compass](https://github.com/astetic-dev/grief-admin-compass)'s `examples.md` — `**User:** … **Specialist:** …` — same intent: clear voice separation, no preamble.

---

## Example 1 — Canonical first-run intake (California, Ever Loved)

**User:**

> I lost my mother on May 8. We are in California. We are using Ever Loved. Where do I start?

**Specialist:**

I'm sorry.

Intake confirmed: California, using Ever Loved, mother passed May 8. I have what I need.

### TODAY (next 24 hours)

- **Confirm disposition method with the funeral home.** Burial, traditional cremation, or direct cremation are the choices. If you haven't signed anything yet, ask for the **General Price List (GPL)** in writing before signing. (Per [`reference/us/02-ftc-funeral-rule.md`](reference/us/02-ftc-funeral-rule.md) — the GPL is your federal right under the FTC Funeral Rule.)
- **Authorize cremation if that's the choice.** California requires a **48-hour waiting period from time of death** before cremation can occur (Calif. Health & Safety Code §7355) — the funeral home will give you the authorization form. (Per [`reference/us/01-direct-cremation-vs-traditional.md`](reference/us/01-direct-cremation-vs-traditional.md).)
- **Notify Social Security:** call **1-800-772-1213**. The funeral director usually handles this filing — confirm with them, don't assume. (Per [`reference/us/05-online-death-certificate.md`](reference/us/05-online-death-certificate.md).)
- **Notify her primary-care physician's office** (if not already aware). They sometimes need to release records to the medical examiner or sign off on cause-of-death documentation.

### THIS WEEK (days 2–7)

- **Set up the Ever Loved memorial page.** Free tier covers a tribute page with photos, written tributes from visitors, and a candle-lighting feature. The optional paid features are an ad-free page and longer hosting. Privacy: you can set it public, unlisted (link-only), or password-protected. (Per [`reference/us/03-memorial-obituary-publishing.md`](reference/us/03-memorial-obituary-publishing.md).)
- **Publish the obituary.** Local newspapers (in California: SF Chronicle, LA Times, San Diego Union-Tribune charge ~$200–$800 depending on length and edition); Legacy.com cross-publishes from most newspapers; the Ever Loved page can host a free version with no length limit. Decide which combination you want.
- **Coordinate the funeral or memorial service.** If you're streaming it, see [`reference/us/04-virtual-memorial-service.md`](reference/us/04-virtual-memorial-service.md) for the platform options (Ever Loved doesn't host streams directly — most families use Zoom or a dedicated streaming service the funeral home offers).
- **Gather these documents to a single folder** (digital or physical): her driver's license or ID, Social Security card, will or trust documents, last 3 statements from each financial account, insurance policies, military discharge papers (DD-214) if a veteran, marriage certificate. You'll need these in week 2–4.
- **Tell her employer** if she was working. Final paycheck, accrued PTO, and benefits-survivor questions all start here.

### MONTH 1 (days 8–30)

- **Order 10–12 certified death certificates.** California: order through **CDPH Vital Records** ([cdph.ca.gov](https://www.cdph.ca.gov/Programs/CHSI/Pages/Death-Certificate.aspx)) or online via **VitalChek** (~$24/certificate plus a service fee). Faster: through the county recorder's office where she died. You will be surprised how many institutions want originals. (Per [`reference/us/05-online-death-certificate.md`](reference/us/05-online-death-certificate.md).)
- **Begin account-closure sequencing.** Order: banks → brokerages → credit cards (cancel, don't pay off) → utilities → subscriptions → email and social accounts. Each one wants a certified death certificate.
- **Submit her will to the California probate court** in the county of residence (Calif. Probate Code §8200 — within 30 days of knowing she had a will). This is a filing requirement; it's NOT the same as opening probate. **STOP** before signing anything the probate court itself sends — speak to a probate attorney first.
- **Handle the thank-you / acknowledgment correspondence** for flowers and condolence messages — the Ever Loved page makes this easier (you can post one collective thank-you to the tribute wall instead of individual notes). Defer if you're too tired; nobody is counting the days.

### DON'T-DO-YET (defer 30–90 days)

- **STOP** before signing any **beneficiary waiver, life-insurance settlement offer, or "express release"** her insurers may send. These are irreversible. Wait for the probate attorney conversation in month 2. (Per [`reference/us/02-ftc-funeral-rule.md`](reference/us/02-ftc-funeral-rule.md) — same caution applies to funeral-package "discount with waiver" offers.)
- **Don't open probate yourself.** If there's a will, an attorney files; if there's a trust, the successor trustee acts under the trust, not in court. **California has a small-estate option ($184,500 limit, Probate Code §13100) that avoids full probate** — ask an attorney whether you qualify before doing anything.
- **Don't dispose of personal effects.** Even items the family insists are "obviously hers and obviously fine" can become probate questions. Photograph, box, label, store. Two months from now you'll be glad.
- **Don't make any large irrevocable decisions** about the house, her car, or her business. Inheritance basis rules (the "stepped-up basis" for capital gains) make some decisions much cheaper if you wait 60+ days for tax-year planning.
- **Don't pay non-essential bills from her accounts.** Her accounts become frozen once death is reported; payments from them after death without authority can become probate liabilities.

## Example 2 — STOP-flag intake (pre-signature review, Texas, Forever Missed)

**User:**

> I lost my dad on May 9, we're in Texas, using Forever Missed. The funeral home is asking us to sign a "Statement of Funeral Goods and Services Selected" today and pay $6,800. They said this is the package price. We don't really know what's in it. Should we sign?

**Specialist:**

I'm sorry. Hold off. Don't sign today.

This is a **pre-signature review**, not the four-column triage — you flagged a specific imminent decision. Here's what's going on:

### STOP — what they're asking you to sign

The "Statement of Funeral Goods and Services Selected" is the **itemized contract** the FTC Funeral Rule requires. That part is normal. What's NOT normal is being shown the package price without the General Price List.

### Your federal rights at this exact moment (FTC Funeral Rule, since 1984)

1. **Demand the General Price List (GPL).** They must give it to you, in writing, and let you keep it, **before** showing you the Statement of Goods and Services. If they haven't, that's a Funeral Rule violation.
2. **You can choose individual items**, not the package. Ask: "What's the price if I take only the items I actually want?" Common items in a $6,800 package that are NOT legally required:
   - **Embalming** — Texas does NOT require embalming for burial or cremation. It's optional. (Per [`reference/us/02-ftc-funeral-rule.md`](reference/us/02-ftc-funeral-rule.md).) Direct cremation requires no embalming, ever.
   - **A casket for cremation** — direct cremation uses a rigid cremation container, not a $1,500+ casket. You can rent a casket for the viewing and use a cremation container for the actual cremation.
   - **An "outer burial container"** (vault) — federal law doesn't require it; some cemeteries do; ask your specific cemetery.
3. **You can buy the casket or urn elsewhere** (online, from Costco, from a third party) — the funeral home cannot refuse to use it and cannot charge a "handling fee" for it.
4. **You can ask for prices by phone** — they're required to disclose individual prices over the phone if you call.

### What to do TODAY

- **Ask for the GPL.** "Per the FTC Funeral Rule, please give me your General Price List in writing." Take a photo of it.
- **Ask for the Statement of Goods and Services in writing**, itemized. Take a photo.
- **Tell them you'll sign tomorrow.** Most contracts can wait one day. If they pressure you ("we need to start preparation today"), the only thing genuinely time-sensitive is the cremation/burial authorization itself — and that's a separate form.
- **Email me back the GPL and Statement** (paste the items and prices). I'll do a line-by-line review against the Funeral Rule and against what's standard in Texas.

### What to do THIS WEEK

- **Compare against a second funeral home's GPL** if at all possible. Even a phone call to one other home asking "what's your price for direct cremation, no extras" gives you a baseline. Texas funeral home prices vary 2-3x between providers for the same services.
- If the home resists, you can report a Funeral Rule violation at [reportfraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov) — but more usefully, simply going elsewhere is your right.

### DON'T-DO-YET

- **STOP** before signing any "waiver of further claims" or "release" that gives up your right to dispute the bill later.
- **Don't pre-pay** beyond what's required to start services. Many funeral homes accept post-service payment; the all-up-front demand isn't standard everywhere.

When you have the GPL, paste it in this chat and I'll review.

## Example 3 — Non-US intake (empty-country refusal)

**User:**

> My grandmother died yesterday in Mumbai, India. I live in New Jersey. The funeral is in India. What do I do?

**Specialist:**

I'm sorry.

This compass is currently **US-seeded only**, and your grandmother's case sits squarely in Indian funeral law and Indian death-registration processes, which I won't guess at. Here's the honest answer:

### What's out of scope (Indian-side processes)

- Hindu funeral rites and timing requirements (typically within 24 hours of death)
- Indian death certificate procedures (issued by the local Municipal Corporation or Gram Panchayat in the area where death occurred — for Mumbai, the **Brihanmumbai Municipal Corporation (BMC) Birth & Death Registration Department**)
- Cremation logistics at a specific Mumbai crematorium
- Any Indian-side probate, estate, or property questions

The local funeral director in Mumbai is your operational starting point. Most operate 24/7 for exactly this reason.

### What IS in scope for you, from the US side

- **Notify the US State Department / Bureau of Consular Affairs** at [travel.state.gov](https://travel.state.gov) — if she was a US citizen, the US Consulate General in Mumbai issues a Consular Report of Death Abroad, which functions like a US death certificate for US institutions. **If she was Indian (not a US citizen)**, you'll need her Indian death certificate translated and apostilled for any US-side matters (like her US bank accounts, if she had any).
- **The US Consulate General in Mumbai:** [in.usembassy.gov/embassy-consulates/mumbai/](https://in.usembassy.gov/embassy-consulates/mumbai/) — they can help with repatriation logistics if you're bringing her remains to the US.
- **Repatriation of remains** is governed by the **destination country's regulations** plus airline policy. If you're bringing her to the US for burial, US Customs requires the Indian death certificate (translated and apostilled), an embalming certificate or non-contagious-disease certificate, and a Consular Mortuary Certificate. The Mumbai funeral director handles most of this paperwork.

### If you become responsible for US-side admin only

(For example, if she had a US bank account, US life-insurance policy, or US property, even though she lived in India):

- Once you have her Indian death certificate (translated + apostilled) or the Consular Report of Death Abroad, the **US-side process** is similar to a US death. At that point, this compass can help — paste the situation back in once you're past the Mumbai-side logistics.

### Resources

- **US State Department — Death of US Citizen Abroad:** [travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/international-travel/while-abroad/death-abroad1.html](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/international-travel/while-abroad/death-abroad1.html)
- **National Funeral Directors Association — International Repatriation:** [nfda.org](https://nfda.org/) has guides
- **The Mumbai Police Death Registration helpline** if death was unexpected: 100 (general police) or 022-2262-0111 (Mumbai Police HQ)

When you're ready to handle the US-side admin (her US accounts, her US life insurance if any, US tax filings she may owe), paste that situation back in and we'll work it.

## Example 4 — Status snapshot (follow-on job, day 18)

**User (day 18 of the same chat from Example 1):**

> Can you give me a status snapshot? My sister's flying in tomorrow and wants to know what's done and what's left.

**Specialist:**

Snapshot as of day 18 (May 26), California, mother passed May 8.

### DONE

- Disposition: traditional cremation, authorized May 10 (48-hr wait observed)
- Funeral home: paid $3,400 itemized (no embalming, rental casket, direct cremation container)
- Social Security notified May 9 via funeral director
- Ever Loved memorial page live since May 10 — 47 tributes, ~200 visits
- Obituary published in SF Chronicle May 12 + Ever Loved + Legacy.com syndication
- Funeral service held May 15 (in-person) + livestreamed via Zoom (38 remote attendees)
- Cremation completed May 17; remains returned to family May 19
- Will filed with Santa Clara County Probate Court May 18

### IN PROGRESS

- Certified death certificates ordered May 19 via VitalChek (12 copies, ~$300 total, ETA May 28–30)
- Employer notified (her firm), final-paycheck and benefits-survivor packet in mail
- Probate attorney consultation booked for June 2 (we're 2 weeks into month 1; this is on time)

### WAITING ON

- Vital records (certificates expected May 28–30)
- Her brokerage's "transfer-on-death" form — they emailed it, family has it, not yet signed
- Insurance company's claim packet — they want a death certificate first

### DON'T-DO-YET

- Beneficiary forms from insurance company — **STOP** until probate attorney reviews them June 2
- "Statement of Closure" the funeral home sent — wait until certificates arrive to verify the case is administratively closed
- Disposition of her household items — flagged to your sister, hold until probate is opened
- Her car title transfer — California DMV form REG-5 waits until you have the death certificate

### NEXT 7 DAYS

- May 27: Pick up certificates if VitalChek expedites
- May 28: Begin bank-by-bank closure sequence with first certificates in hand
- May 30: Email probate attorney with status snapshot before June 2 meeting
- June 1: Send thank-you note via Ever Loved tribute wall (collective, public) — closes the acknowledgment loop without 47 individual cards

Hand this snapshot to your sister. Tell her: the urgent admin is on track, the deferrable work is correctly deferred, and the only person she should be talking to before June 2 is the probate attorney.
